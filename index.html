Finance manager summary — March 2026
The office closed March 2026 with a net surplus of $1.25M, driven by strong Global Centre and Direct Funding receipts. However, three areas require management attention: cash position remains 40% below the target low at $301K (vs. $499K target); admin cost rate has crept to 10.4% against a 9.49% target, primarily from technical support and CLU cost overruns; and ADP Bukidnon is the only project in overspend at 109% YTD driven by shared NO/FO costs rather than direct programme costs. The majority of underspending across other projects is attributable to rescheduled activities, integration savings, and deferred implementation — all with catch-up plans in place for Q2.
⚠ Items requiring management action
Overspend

Shared technical & management costs (NO/FO) are the primary driver. Programme costs remain within limits. Monitor monthly to prevent year-end overspend.

Cost reduction in support costs is ongoing. BIR tax exemption threshold (30%) is not at risk, but internal target must be met by year-end.

Environment & risk highlights
Internal and external factors affecting financial position
Forex
4.2% increase in USD/PHP revaluation rate (58.33 vs. 56.00 budgeted), causing underspending of USD-funded budgets. PBAS USD allocations to be adjusted at year-end based on actual exchange rates.
Inflation
March 2026 inflation at 4.1%. Middle East crisis could push inflation to 5%–7.3% and weaken peso to ₱60–₱61.30/USD, reducing purchasing power and requiring stricter cost controls.
Burn rate
Aggregate 93% YTD / 41% of annual budget. Key factors: rescheduled trainings (SNC, CEW, PEACE clubs), activity integration savings, deferred AIM+ and tablet procurement, overspending in technical support & CLU functions.
